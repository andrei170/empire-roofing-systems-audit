How fast someone rings back. Subcontract gangs move between firms as work finishes; a gang that applies on Tuesday and hears nothing until Friday has usually gone to someone else. Same-day callback is free and it is the biggest lever you have.

How We Would Actually Target It
First, the thing most agencies will not tell you

You cannot run a hiring campaign on search keywords alone in this trade. Almost nobody types "flat roofer jobs Kettering" into Google in any real volume — there simply are not enough people doing it in a given week to fill six to eight seats.

So the campaign is not built on what people search for. It is built on where the people you want already are, and put in front of them whether or not they were looking that morning. Most of the roofers you want to hire are not actively job hunting. They are in work, they are fed up, and they have not done anything about it yet.

The sniper version, for hiring
  • Geography drawn round the places roofers physically are. Builders' merchants, roofing supplies depots and trade counters in each of your three regions — tight radii, not a blanket county.
  • Targeted by trade, not by job hunting. People whose profiles and behaviour put them in roofing and construction, including those following the manufacturer and supplier brands you already work with.
  • Filtered hard on the way in. Negative terms and exclusions so you are not paying to reach people looking for a roof rather than a job, and not paying twice for people already in your pipeline.
  • Retargeting, which matters more here than anywhere. Somebody who reads your hiring page and does not fill the form in is the single warmest audience you will ever have. They thought about it. They will see you again next week, when their current job has had another bad day.
  • Separated by business and by region, so you can see which of the three is producing applicants and which is not — and move budget between them rather than guessing.

The point of the sniper approach is not cheap clicks. It is refusing to pay for the wrong people — and in recruitment advertising the wrong people are the overwhelming majority of any untargeted audience.

And the free lever, which beats all of the above

How fast you ring back. A roofer who leaves a number on a Tuesday evening and hears nothing until Friday has usually stopped caring by Wednesday — and in the meantime somebody else rang them.

That is why instant notification is built into every campaign rather than sold as an extra. No amount of budget compensates for a slow callback, and no agency can fix it for you.

Why Three Is Priced The Way It Is
Not a discount for the sake of one

Most of the work in a first campaign is thinking — who the applicant is, what actually appeals to a roofer, what the advert has to say, how the tracking should be laid out. That thinking is done once and the second and third campaigns inherit it.

So the third genuinely costs us less to build, and the saving goes to you rather than into our margin. It also happens to be the right thing to do commercially for you: three campaigns running at once means three sets of data, and the one that works best tells you what to change in the other two.

That said, the urgent need you described is the flat roofing company, so that is the campaign we would start with. If the other two businesses need people later in the year, they can be added — and if all three are commissioned together, the third is still free.
