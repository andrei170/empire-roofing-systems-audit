This Is Not A Lead Generation Audit
Because that is not what you said the problem was

You told our team you own three businesses, you want to hire six to eight people across them, and you are struggling to find them. So we have not written you a document about getting more enquiries. More enquiries would make your problem worse, not better.

Recruitment is a marketing problem. It uses the same machinery as customer advertising — a page built to convert, traffic pointed at it, and tracking so you know which source produced the person who actually turned up. The only thing that changes is who you are talking to and what you are offering them.

The good news is in the competitor analysis: nobody in your local market is doing this either. This is an open lane, not a race you are losing.

Finding 1 — There Is Nowhere For A Roofer To Land
Every page on empireroofingsystems.com, read from the site itself: / Home /about-us About Us /services Services /gallery Gallery /contact-us Contact Us /commercial-roofing Commercial Roofing /installation Flat Roof Installation /privacy-policy Privacy Policy Pages about joining the team ......... 0 Pages naming a rate, a van, or progression ... 0 The word "careers" anywhere .......... 0
Why this matters more than it sounds

Experienced flat roofers are not short of options. When one is deciding whether to answer your advert, they look you up — and what they find is a site that talks exclusively to building owners. Nothing about the work, the kit, the guarantees you stand behind, who they would be working alongside, or why Empire is a better seat than the firm they are in now.

You have a genuinely good story and it is not written down anywhere. NFRC membership, manufacturer guarantees up to thirty years, commercial and industrial work, three offices, drone survey photography. To a roofer that reads as a serious outfit with real contracts. At the moment none of it is aimed at them.

Finding 2 — Your Homepage Is Still Carrying Template Text
The main body text of your homepage, exactly as published: "Empire Roofing Systems Fully Insured Flat Roofing Specialists Empire Roofing Systems Fully Insured Flat Roofing Specialists Empire Roofing Systems Fully Insured Flat Roofing Specialists" The same sentence, three times. That is the homepage copy.
What happened here

This is a GoDaddy website builder default. The template repeats the headline into the body slots and expects you to overwrite them. It was never overwritten.

So the single most visited page on your website says nothing about what you do, who you do it for, or why anyone should call — to a customer or to a roofer. Everything good about Empire is on the About and Services pages, which most visitors never reach.

Finding 3 — Two Of Your Phone Links Are Broken
On your commercial roofing page, the click-to-call links are published as: tel:+4401536 520 908 <- +44 followed by a 0, and a space inside the number tel:+101536520908 <- +1 is the United States dialling code What a correctly formatted UK link looks like: tel:+441536520908 <- +44, drop the leading 0, no spaces
This is a real lost-call problem, not a cosmetic one

Most people reading a roofing website are on a phone, and the way they call you is by tapping the number. A malformed tel: link either fails silently or dials the wrong thing. The second one prefixes a United States country code onto a Kettering landline.

Nothing about this shows up as an error anywhere. The page looks fine. The number looks fine. It simply does not always connect, and you would never know which calls you did not get.

The plain version

You have three offices and three phone numbers. Two much smaller competitors put "Kettering" into nearly every page address on their sites. You have the wider footprint and they have the local signal.

This matters for hiring as well as for work. A roofer searching for a job near Birmingham does not find a Birmingham page on your site, because there is not one.

Finding 5 — Your NFRC Membership Is A Picture
The strongest thing you have, in the weakest possible format

You are a member of the National Federation of Roofing Contractors. On your site that appears as a logo image in a strip of logos, plus one sentence in the middle of the About page.

A logo is a picture. Google cannot read a picture. Neither can somebody skim-reading on a phone. For a commercial buyer specifying a flat roof, NFRC membership is often a hard requirement on the tender — and for a roofer choosing an employer it says the firm is inspected and takes standards seriously.

It should be in a heading, in words, on the homepage. It is currently doing almost none of the work it could do.

Worth knowing: the NFRC runs a free careers and skills service for member employers. You are already paying for the membership. Not assessed whether you have used it — but given what you told us about hiring, it is worth ten minutes.

Finding 6 — You Sell Maintenance Packages In Four Words
From your services list, in full: Built Up Felt Roofing Systems / Warm Roofs Flame Free Felt Roofing Systems New Installations Eco Friendly Green Roofs Liquid Coating Systems All Roofing Repairs Lead Work Facias & Guttering Maintenance Packages Available <- the last line of a bullet list
Why we are pointing at this one

A maintenance package is recurring, scheduled, planned work. It is the kind of revenue you can staff against, because you know it is coming and when. For a business trying to justify six to eight new hires, predictable work is worth more than a bigger pile of one-off enquiries.

At the moment it is the ninth bullet in a list, with no page, no explanation of what is included, and no way to ask for one.

What We Would Do, In Order
  • Fix the two phone links. Ten minutes. It is the only item on this page that is losing you calls today.
  • Write the homepage. Replace the repeated sentence with what Empire actually is: NFRC-member flat roofing, commercial and domestic, three offices, guarantees to thirty years.
  • Build one careers page and make it good. The rate, the kit, the type of work, the guarantees the firm stands behind, who they would report to, and a form that takes a name and a number and nothing else. This is the page the whole hiring push points at.
  • Point paid traffic at it. Recruitment advertising is cheap compared with customer advertising in this trade, because almost nobody bids on it. Same machinery, different audience.
  • Put tracking on. Without it you cannot tell which source produced the roofer who actually started, which means you cannot spend more on the one that worked.
  • Add city pages for Kettering, Birmingham and London. For work and for hiring.
  • Give maintenance packages a page. Predictable, schedulable revenue is what makes six to eight hires survivable.
